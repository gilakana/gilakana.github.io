# Requirements:
The vision for the website:
I want to build a website that serves as a professional portfolio for my artworks.
I want there to be a menu in the top left corner that the user can expand with the following categories: 'Home' and 'Recent works'. The menu icon should remain fixed in the top-left corner of the screen while the user scrolls.
The aim of the website is for it to serve as an introduction to me and how I work. 
Pages should scroll vertically when content exceeds the viewport height. Do not force page content to fit entirely within one screen.
Artwork images should be displayed at high visual quality while being appropriately optimised for web loading speed.

At the top of every page I want there to be my signature in the middle of a white banner. 
A footer should appear at the end of the content on every page with a subheading 'CONNECT'and below it : 'Find out more about me and my works in progress by following me on Instagram.' Below this there will be in regular text 'INSTAGRAM' which will be a live link to an instagram account, and below that regular text 'EMAIL' which will be a live link to the gmail address. The footer should have generous spacing above and below it and should not feel crowded against the preceding artwork/content.
Instagram account: @gila_kana
Gmail address: gilakana.art@gmail.com
I will have an expandable menu. once the menu icon is clicked, a partially-transparent box will appear taking up 1/4 of the screen horizontally and the whole screen vertically which will be the menu, with text 'Home' and 'Recent works' acting as links to take user to separate pages.The menu should close when the menu icon is clicked again, when the user selects a menu link, or when the user clicks outside the menu panel. The menu overlay should cover the entire viewport and remain above all page content. The menu panel should occupy approximately 25% of the viewport width on desktop and an appropriately wider proportion on mobile (approximately 70–85% of the screen width) so that the navigation remains comfortable to use.

On the 'Home' page, I want my signature to appear as a horizontal signature image across the top on a white banner.Banner height differs by page. In the Home page, the white banner will take up about half of the screen space vertically. The white banner spans the full width of the page, with the signature image horizontally centred within it. The signature should occupy approximately 80–85% of the banner's vertical height, leaving a small amount of white space above and below it. The signature image must maintain its original aspect ratio and must never be stretched or distorted. Below this I will have quite big size text for the first part of the tagline heading and smaller size text for tagline subheading. Below this text, in smaller size text, I will have a short artist statement graphy. 
The artist statement should occupy a broad, centred text area with generous margins on both sides. It should not appear as a narrow newspaper-style column. On smaller screens, the text area should automatically reduce in width while retaining comfortable horizontal margins. Use generous line spacing so the artist statement graphy remains easy to read and does not appear as a dense block of text. Preserve the three paragraph breaks in the artist statement.
Text for tagline heading: Painting with flowers. 
Text for tagline subheading: A ritual of remembrance. 
Display 'Painting with flowers.' and 'A ritual of remembrance.' as two visually distinct typographic elements.
Text for artist statement graphy ('painting with flowers' to be in italics):
Through my art, I seek to express a deep admiration, love, and appreciation for nature in a world that has become increasingly disconnected from it. Grounded in my undergraduate research on environmental conservation and climate justice, my works reflect nature’s inherent value, constant movement, and organised chaos. My art is a direct challenge to the Anthropocene and Capitalocene, replacing consumption with connection and celebrating humans’ divine union with nature. 
At the heart of my practice is what I call ‘painting with flowers.’ Flowers often form the very soul of my paintings, with their mesmerising beauty, unique textures and bright colours. Having responsibly handpicked, dried, and pressed each one myself, I give them eternal life on the canvas.
Working primarily in acrylics, I paint worlds at the intersection of dreams, feeling, and reality. My pieces are rich with symbolism, deeply layered in colour, and inspired by the interconnectedness of human culture, mythology, and the natural world. My compositions invite the viewer to look more closely and notice the subtle relationships that often go overlooked.


In the 'Recent works' page the signature image should appear in a thinner horizontal banner than on the Home page, with the banner occupying approximately one eighth of the screen height.Below that will be a heading 'Recent works'. Below this I will have photos of my artworks. Display artworks in a responsive three-column grid on desktop, with remaining artworks continuing onto subsequent rows. Display the artworks at a visually consistent scale while preserving the original aspect ratio of each artwork. Do not crop or distort the artwork images. When the viewer hovers their mouse over a photo of the artwork it, a partially transparent box fit to the shape and size of the photo of the artwork will appear while the artwork beneath it is still visible. Below the photo will be a title of the work. In the partially transparent box will be text 'About the work:'a line break and below it display a short, consistent excerpt from each description, limited to approximately 3–4 lines, followed by '…Read more'. Only the phrase 'Read more' is clickable and will act as a link which when clicked will take the user to a separate page of that individual artwork. Centre the overlay text both horizontally and vertically within the overlay. Do not allow the overlay text to touch the edges of the artwork. Maintain comfortable internal padding.
On desktop devices with a mouse, artwork hover interactions should be used as described. On touch devices such as phones and tablets, the same information should be accessible through tapping. Hover interactions must never be the only way to access important information or navigation. On touch devices, tapping the artwork should reveal the overlay without immediately navigating away from the page. Tapping “Read more” should then open the individual artwork page.

On the page of the individual artwork, for each artwork, there will be the same signature and banner, both in the same proporitons as in 'Recent works' page across the top of the page, the same space the same photo of the artwork as in 'Recent works' on the left hand side but occupying about half of the  horizontal length of the page. On smaller screens, the two-column layout should become a single-column layout, with the artwork image appearing above the artwork description. Below the banner, the title of the artwork should appear as a centred heading. When a user hovers their mouse over the artwork, the image will become enlarged, enabling the user to see the details of the photo. The image should open in a full-screen lightbox that allows the user to zoom in and inspect fine details. On touch devices, users should be able to pinch to zoom. To the right, there will be the heading 'About this work' and below the heading there will be the full description of the work (a part of which was already visable to the user when the mouse hovered on a photo in the 'Recent works' page). Below the photo on the left hand side will be the year the work was created, the dimensions of the work, and the medium.

Artwork 1 
title: Divine Couple: Conception - A Place Where Dreams Are Born 
year: 2023 
dimensions: 18cm x 13cm x 1.5cm
medium: mixed media
description:
The woman holds a lily in her hands while the man declares his love for her - is he singing or comparing her beauty to that of the sky? 
The first in a series of paintings inspired by Greek signet rings and my first painting to experiment with dried flowers. The ‘Divine Couple Ring’ served as inspiration for this piece. 
In the background on the left lies an ancient burial mound of the Ukrainian steppes, and on the right stands the Chimborazo mountain of Ecuador. A river and sunflowers push the two worlds together, uniting their dreams. 
Set at dawn, yet shining with bright colours, I like to think of it as an evening painting.

Artwork 2 
title: Divine Couple: Crossing - The Path Unfolds
year: 2026
dimensions: 18cm x 13cm x 1.5cm 
medium: mixed media
description:
This is the second painting inspired by Greek signet rings. Once again featuring the Divine Couple, they are shown at the point of crossing the river in a boat.
The boat and fish are made of multiple individual flower petals, and the bouquet in the woman’s hands is made of many different flowers. Gold paint was used for the dominant elements of the original ring.  A strawberry acts as the moon, inspired by my dream.
The woman initially seemed reluctant to go. But upon testing the arrangement of flowers on the canvas, I saw that she was instead intrigued by the bouquet, looking down at it fully. 
I like to think of it as a daytime painting.


Artwork 3
title (including the number 107): 107 Butterfly Effect
year: 2026
dimensions: 23cm x 21cm x 0.3cm 
medium: acrylic on canvas
description:
This piece explores moments of manifestation. The title comes from a moment when, after focusing intensely on the number 107, it began appearing around me in unexpected places later that day.
A similar experience arose after talking about butterflies with my partner in detail, describing the colours and patterns of their wings. The very next day, I saw the exact same butterfly we had discussed.
A few days after completing this piece, I went on a trip and saw the exact same low-hanging clouds I had painted, including the heart shapes nestled between them.



Artwork 4
title: El Encuentro
year: 2026
dimensions: 13cm x 18cm x 0.3cm
medium: mixed media
description: 
'El Encuentro' is a mind game. It is a painting that appears chaotic at first glance, but gains its order upon close inspection. The story unfolds depending on how you classify elements of the painting: do they exist in the physical, spiritual, or both realms? The answer lies in the water reflections (or their absence).
